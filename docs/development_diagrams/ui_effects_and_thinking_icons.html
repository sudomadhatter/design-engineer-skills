1

Say it in one sentence

Every design change fits one sentence with five slots. Fill all five and the request can only mean one thing. Leave one out and whoever builds it has to guess, and a guess is how a two-minute change turns into three rounds.

1 · WHERE
Device, theme, state
"On a phone, in dark mode, while it's thinking"
2 · WHAT
Screen, then component
"on the dashboard, the chat window"
3 · WHICH LAYER
The one part you mean
"the edge ring", "the out glow" (see 2)
4 · WHICH KNOB
What about it changes
strength, reach, softness, speed, color, motion (see 3)
5 · HOW MUCH
Direction and amount
"to half of today" or "to 50%", never just "in half"
WORKED EXAMPLE · REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY · AVCH-197
WHAT WAS ASKED

"For the dark mode on mobile we turned down the size-pulse-outside by 30%. It's still too overpowering. Let's cut it in half."

WHY IT TOOK THREE ROUNDS

"Size-pulse-outside" names the whole halo, not a layer: the code's setting is literally size="pulse-outside". The halo has three layers, and "cut it in half" could be strength or reach, half of today's 70% or half of full. That's a dozen honest readings, and the first one planned was the wrong one.

THE SAME REQUEST, IN THIS VOCABULARY

"On a phone, in dark mode, on the Chuck, HR and Specialist chat windows: edge ring to 50% strength; out glow to 50% strength and 60% reach; outer bloom back to 100%."

This is the version that shipped.

Name the layer, not the effect. "The halo" is three things. "The out glow" is one.
Name the knob. "Dimmer" is strength. "Smaller" is reach. "Softer" is blur. "Calmer" is usually speed.
Anchor every percent. Say "half of today" or "to 50% strength". "Cut it in half" is both.
Don't know the name? Describe it and point. "The thin line on the edge" or a screenshot with a circle works. Claude maps it to the word here.

Anatomy of a glow: the layers you can name

A glowing window is a stack of separate layers, and each one can be changed on its own. The same stack looks different on a phone, because a phone's chat window fills the screen: everything past the window's edge is off the glass, so on a phone you mostly see the edge ring and the part of the out glow that shines in through the window.

4

Words that get mixed up

Each pair below sounds like one thing and is two. These are the ones that cost rounds.

Out glow vs inner glow

An out glow sits behind the element and shines out past its edge. An inner glow stays inside the edge. On the halo, the code's "core" and "inner-opacity" names mean the out glow; on the traveling beams the same slot really is an inner glow.

Strength vs reach

"Turn it down" can mean dimmer (strength) or smaller (reach). Say which. You usually want both a little rather than one a lot.

size="…" vs size

In the Border Beam code, size is the effect's name (pulse-outside, line, sm). How big it is lives under reach.

Percent of today vs percent strength

At 70% today, "half" is 35% or 50%. Say "half of today" or "to 50%".

Padding vs margin vs gap

Padding is space inside a box, between its edge and its content. Margin is space outside it, pushing neighbors away. Gap is the space between items in a row or grid.

Border vs outline vs focus ring

A border is part of the box and takes up space. An outline is drawn outside it and takes up none. A focus ring is the outline that shows where the keyboard is.

Shadow vs glow

A shadow is dark and falls below or behind, so the thing looks raised. A glow is light and spreads all around, so the thing looks lit.

Blur vs frost (backdrop blur)

Blur softens the thing itself. Frost softens what is behind a see-through layer, the glass effect.

Opacity vs brightness

Lower opacity makes it see-through. Lower brightness makes it darker but still solid.

Modal vs drawer vs sheet vs popover

A modal sits over the middle and blocks everything. A drawer slides in from a side. A sheet rises from the bottom on a phone. A popover floats beside the thing that opened it and blocks nothing.

Toast vs banner vs tooltip

A toast pops in and leaves on its own. A banner is a strip that stays until closed. A tooltip is a tiny label that shows on hover or long-press.

Hover vs focus vs pressed

Hover is the mouse over it, and phones don't have one. Focus is where the keyboard or text cursor is. Pressed is the moment of the tap or click.

Transition vs animation

A transition plays once, when something changes, like fading in when opened. An animation plays on its own, often on a loop, like breathing.

Viewport vs window

The viewport is the visible area of the browser, the whole screen on a phone. A window, in our apps, is a panel inside the page, like the Chuck chat window.

Screen sizes: where the layout changes

A breakpoint is a screen width where the layout changes. We build mobile first: the phone is the base design, and each wider size adds to it. Widths are in CSS pixels. A 375px phone is an iPhone-sized screen, whatever its hardware pixels.

6

The dictionary: every UI word, grouped

Plain meanings for the words designers and code use, in every project. The gray tag after a term is what it's called in code.

Screen & layout

Viewport: the visible part of the browser. On a phone, the whole screen.
Breakpoint: a width where the layout changes (see 5).
Mobile first: design the phone first; wider screens add to it.
Full-bleed: runs edge to edge with no side space.
Container max-width: the invisible column that stops content stretching too wide on big screens.
Gutter: the side space between content and the screen edge. House phones: 16px.
Padding · margin · gap: space inside, outside, and between (see 4).
Stack · row · grid: items piled top to bottom, side by side, or in cells.
Alignment: where items sit in their space: start, center, end, or stretched.
Safe area: the part of a phone screen clear of the notch and the home bar.
Above the fold: visible without scrolling.
Sticky · fixed: stays put while you scroll. Sticky holds only within its section; fixed holds always.

Surfaces: the boxes things live in

Screen · page: one full view you navigate to.
Section: a band of the page with one job.
Card: a bordered box holding one thing.
Panel: a larger region, often a side column.
Window (in-app): a card that works like a mini app, such as a chat window.
Modal · dialog: a box over the page that blocks the rest until it closes.
Drawer: slides in from an edge and stays attached to it.
Sheet: a drawer that rises from the bottom, on phones.
Popover: a small floating box beside what opened it.
Tooltip · toast · banner: hover label, pop-in message, stay-put strip (see 4).
Chip · pill: a small rounded tag or option.
Badge: a tiny marker on something else, like a count or a status dot.
Tabs · accordion: switch views in one place / sections that open and close.

Edges & shape

Border stroke: the line around an element.
Hairline: a 1px border, the thinnest there is.
Divider: a line separating sections.
Corner radius rounded: how round the corners are. 0 is square; full is a pill.
Outline · focus ring: lines drawn outside the border (see 4).
Edge ring: a glowing line on the border (see 2).

Depth & layers

Layer: anything stacked on top of something else.
Stacking order z-index: which layer is on top.
Elevation: how raised it looks. Higher means a bigger, softer shadow.
Drop shadow · inner shadow inset: shadow cast outside it, so it looks raised / shadow inside the edge, so it looks pressed in.
Overlay · scrim: the dimming layer over the page behind a modal.
Backdrop: whatever sits behind a see-through layer.
Glass: a see-through surface. Tint is how dark or colored it is; frost is how much it blurs what's behind.

Color & light

Token: a named color or value used everywhere. Change it once and it changes everywhere.
Palette: the approved set of colors (Ocean, Sunset).
Primary · accent: the main brand color / the highlight color for actions.
Opacity alpha: how solid. 0% is invisible, 100% is solid.
Hue · saturation · brightness: which color, how vivid, how light.
Gradient: a smooth blend between colors. Linear runs in a line; radial runs out from a center.
Contrast: how far text stands out from what's behind it. Body text needs 4.5 to 1; large text needs 3 to 1.
Glow · shadow: light spreading out / dark falling behind (see 4).

Type

Typeface · font: the letter design.
Size scale: the fixed set of text sizes. Pick from it; don't invent one.
Weight: how thick, from regular to medium to bold.
Line height leading: space between lines.
Letter spacing tracking: space between letters.
Hierarchy: heading, subheading, body, caption, from loudest to quietest.
Truncation: cutting long text off with "…".
Monospace: every letter the same width, for code and labels.

Motion

Transition · animation: plays once on a change / plays on its own (see 4).
Duration: how long one run or cycle takes.
Easing: how it speeds up and slows down. Ease-out starts fast and lands gently, the default for things appearing.
Spring: overshoots a touch and settles, like a real object.
Delay · stagger: wait before starting / start items one after another.
Fade · slide · scale: appear by opacity, by moving in, by growing.
Breathe · orbit · sweep · shimmer: swell and fade / travel around the edge / glide back and forth / a glint passing across.
Drift: a slow color shift over time.
Reduced motion: the phone setting that asks apps to calm or stop motion. We honor it.

States

Default rest: nothing happening.
Hover · focus · pressed: mouse over / keyboard or cursor on / being tapped (see 4).
Selected: chosen, and stays chosen.
Disabled: visible but can't be used.
Loading · empty · error · success: working / nothing to show yet / something failed / it worked.

Waiting & feedback

Spinner: a looping sign that something is working.
Skeleton: gray placeholder shapes where content will land.
Progress bar: how far along something is.
Thinking indicator: the animated icon, a thinking spinner, while an AI reasons. All nine live in 7b, named by shape: Rubik's cube, orbits, globe, wave, constellation, braid, ribbon, ring, morph.
Typing indicator · streaming: the dots while someone types / text appearing as it's written.

Theme

Dark mode · light mode: light text on dark / dark text on light.
System: follows the phone's own setting, and flips when the phone does.
Daylight (project-specific): custom name for a light theme variant.
